- Basilar/subpleural reticulation without honeycombing (possible UIP criteria) (1)

- Basilar/subpleural reticulation distribution (1)
- Reticulation in basal and subpleural distribution (1)

- Absence of features suggesting non-IPF diagnosis (1)
- Peripheral distribution of pathological tissue (lower-peripheral lung regions) (1)
- High extent of reticulation on CNN tissue characterization (1)
- Peripheral axial distribution (73.3% of pathologic UIP cases) (1)

## Common weak or non-specific signals from the broader supporting layer
- Mean lung attenuation (weakest single predictor among histogram features) (1)
- Corrected mean lung attenuation (lower correlation than uncorrected MLA) (1)
- Diffusing lung capacity showed least correlation with histogram features (1)
- Histogram features insensitive to textural changes such as ground-glass, reticular abnormality, and honeycombing (1)
- Annual rate of FVC decline (~193 mL/year non-IPF vs ~221 mL/year IPF, not significantly different) (1)
- Proportion with FVC decline >10% predicted at 52 weeks (~49% in both groups) (1)
- Mortality over 52 weeks (5.1% non-IPF vs 7.8% IPF, not significantly different in overall population) (1)
- FVC decline >10% predicted associated with increased mortality risk in both IPF and non-IPF (HR ~3.6 vs ~4.0) (1)
- UIP-like fibrotic pattern on HRCT associated with faster FVC decline in both IPF and non-IPF (1)
- DLCO % predicted similar between groups (~47-48%) (1)
- Inconsistent with UIP pattern on HRCT (PPV only 22.7% for UIP, insufficient to distinguish) (1)
- Smoking history (ever vs never) - not selected by LASSO as significant predictor (1)

## Bottom-line interpretation rule
For final IPF-vs-Other case classification:
1. Give highest weight to direct comparator evidence from the direct core layer.
2. Use the broader supporting layer to interpret radiomics and clinical patterns, not to overrule direct evidence.
3. Distinguish IPF-specific evidence from non-specific fibrotic ILD abnormality.
4. Prefer coherent multi-feature and multi-modal evidence over isolated deviations.
5. Reduce confidence when the case relies mainly on weak, unstable, or non-specific features.
