Title: Classification of Interstitial Lung Abnormality Patterns with an Ensemble of Deep Convolutional Neural Networks
One-sentence takeaway: An ensemble of 2D/2.5D/3D CNNs trained on smoker CT data achieves AUC 0.863 for distinguishing ILA-positive from ILA-negative subjects, demonstrating that CNN-learned texture patterns (ground-glass, reticular, nodular, linear scar, subpleural line) can separate early interstitial abnormalities from normal parenchyma, though the smoker-only population and lack of explicit radiomic features limit direct applicability to general ILD-vs-healthy classification.
Population summary: 208 CT scans from the COPDGene study (smokers); 37,427 manually labeled tissue sample points across 8 parenchymal classes including normal parenchyma, 5 interstitial patterns, and 2 emphysematous patterns. Clinical validation on 114 subjects (52 with ILA, 62 without ILA).

Weak/non-specific features: Ground-glass pattern (very few training samples: 137 points, 9 scans); Nodular pattern (very few training samples: 116 points, 7 scans); Linear scar pattern (very few training samples: 195 points, 8 scans); Subpleural line pattern (relatively few training samples: 413 points, 19 scans); Reticular pattern sometimes misclassified as subpleural line or nodular; Normal parenchyma sometimes confused with centrilobular emphysema; Features learned from smoker cohort only, limiting generalizability to non-smoker ILD
Interpretation notes: Features are learned by deep CNNs (not hand-crafted radiomics), so individual feature interpretability is limited; Grad-CAM activation maps confirm CNNs focus on lesion locations, not background; ILA score defined as percentage of lung volume classified as any interstitial pattern with certainty >95%; AUC of 0.863 for detecting visually defined ILA vs. non-ILA in 114 subjects; Normal parenchyma class was labeled both adjacent to and distant from lesions, only in clearly normal areas; CNN features capture local texture patterns at secondary pulmonary lobule scale (~30mm ROI); Sharp reconstruction kernel (B50) yielded better classification performance than soft kernel (B35); Population is exclusively smokers from COPDGene; ILA may represent pre-ILD rather than established ILD
2. (P070 | score=0.152147) Paper ID: P070
Title: Classification of parenchymal abnormality in scleroderma lung using a novel approach to denoise images collected via a multicenter study
One-sentence takeaway: Denoised CT texture features (statistical, run-length, co-occurrence) classified normal lung tissue versus scleroderma ILD patterns with improved accuracy using SVM, particularly for distinguishing normal from ground-glass opacity, though normal lung ROIs came from ILD patients rather than healthy controls.
Population summary: Scleroderma patients from the multicenter NIH-sponsored Scleroderma Lung Study (SLS); HRCT scans acquired across 13 clinical centers on 13 scanner models; ROIs labeled as lung fibrosis (LF), ground-glass (GG), honeycomb (HC), and normal lung (NL)
ILD-supporting features: Statistical texture features distinguishing LF, GG, HC from NL; Run-length parameters elevated in fibrotic and ground-glass regions; Co-occurrence parameters differentiating abnormal parenchymal patterns; 58 texture features computed per pixel; 45 selected (original) or 38 selected (denoised) via multinomial logistic regression for disease classification
Healthy-supporting features: Denoised images show more uniform attenuation in NL tissue (more homogeneous pixel values); NL tissue appears more uniformly dark after denoising, reflecting absence of pathological texture; Fewer oscillatory texture patterns in NL regions after denoising; NL correctly classified at 89.7% ROI-level using denoised texture features
Weak/non-specific features: Honeycomb (HC) texture features showed poor classification rates (<20%) and were not improved by denoising; Features derived from original (non-denoised) images showed higher false-positive rates for NL classification; Pixel-level features susceptible to noise variation across scanner platforms and dose protocols; 2D-only features; 3D texture features not computed due to 10mm slice intervals
Interpretation notes: Denoising with Aujol's algorithm reduced noise-driven texture variability, improving NL classification most substantially; NL tissue in scleroderma patients is typically more central, where noise is higher, making denoising especially beneficial for NL discrimination; GG patterns can be mimicked by image noise, leading to false positives when classifying NL vs. GG without denoising; SVM with radial basis kernel used for final classification after multinomial logistic regression feature selection; Normal lung tissue classification improved from 75.9% to 89.7% at ROI level after denoising; Features were not validated against pathological ground truth; ROIs selected for classic, unambiguous appearance only
