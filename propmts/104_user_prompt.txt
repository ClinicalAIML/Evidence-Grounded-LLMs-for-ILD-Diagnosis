Title: Classification of Interstitial Lung Abnormality Patterns with an Ensemble of Deep Convolutional Neural Networks
One-sentence takeaway: An ensemble of 2D/2.5D/3D CNNs trained on smoker CT data achieves AUC 0.863 for distinguishing ILA-positive from ILA-negative subjects, demonstrating that CNN-learned texture patterns (ground-glass, reticular, nodular, linear scar, subpleural line) can separate early interstitial abnormalities from normal parenchyma, though the smoker-only population and lack of explicit radiomic features limit direct applicability to general ILD-vs-healthy classification.
Population summary: 208 CT scans from the COPDGene study (smokers); 37,427 manually labeled tissue sample points across 8 parenchymal classes including normal parenchyma, 5 interstitial patterns, and 2 emphysematous patterns. Clinical validation on 114 subjects (52 with ILA, 62 without ILA).
ILD-supporting features: Ground-glass opacity pattern (CNN-learned features); Reticular pattern (CNN-learned features); Nodular pattern (CNN-learned features); Linear scar pattern (CNN-learned features); Subpleural line pattern (CNN-learned features); High percentage of lung volume classified as interstitial pattern (ILA score); High-certainty voxel probability for interstitial labels
Healthy-supporting features: Normal parenchyma classification (CNN-learned features); Low percentage of lung volume affected by interstitial patterns; Low certainty scores for interstitial pattern labels
Weak/non-specific features: Ground-glass pattern (very few training samples: 137 points, 9 scans); Nodular pattern (very few training samples: 116 points, 7 scans); Linear scar pattern (very few training samples: 195 points, 8 scans); Subpleural line pattern (relatively few training samples: 413 points, 19 scans); Reticular pattern sometimes misclassified as subpleural line or nodular; Normal parenchyma sometimes confused with centrilobular emphysema; Features learned from smoker cohort only, limiting generalizability to non-smoker ILD
Interpretation notes: Features are learned by deep CNNs (not hand-crafted radiomics), so individual feature interpretability is limited; Grad-CAM activation maps confirm CNNs focus on lesion locations, not background; ILA score defined as percentage of lung volume classified as any interstitial pattern with certainty >95%; AUC of 0.863 for detecting visually defined ILA vs. non-ILA in 114 subjects; Normal parenchyma class was labeled both adjacent to and distant from lesions, only in clearly normal areas; CNN features capture local texture patterns at secondary pulmonary lobule scale (~30mm ROI); Sharp reconstruction kernel (B50) yielded better classification performance than soft kernel (B35); Population is exclusively smokers from COPDGene; ILA may represent pre-ILD rather than established ILD
2. (P094 | score=0.139877) Paper ID: P094
Title: MUC5B variant is associated with visually and quantitatively detected preclinical pulmonary fibrosis
One-sentence takeaway: A deep learning CNN texture-based quantitative HRCT fibrosis score (AUC 0.92) can distinguish preclinical pulmonary fibrosis from healthy lung in high-risk FIP relatives, with older age, male sex, smoking, and MUC5B variant as key associated risk factors, though the tool has high negative predictive value (97%) but low positive predictive value (46%) and was not validated in a general population.
Population summary: 494 non-Hispanic white first-degree relatives of Familial Interstitial Pneumonia (FIP) patients aged >40 years, screened for preclinical pulmonary fibrosis (PrePF) via HRCT. 100 COPDGene never-smoker normal controls were included for quantitative CT comparison. Mean age 57 years, 38% male, 30% ever-smokers.
ILD-supporting features: Higher CNN-based quantitative HRCT fibrosis score (% lung volume classified as fibrotic); Reticular abnormality on CT; Honeycombing on CT; Traction bronchiectasis on CT; Lower-lobe predominant subpleural fibrotic changes consistent with UIP pattern; Higher percent high attenuation area (%HAA) on HRCT
Healthy-supporting features: Low CNN fibrosis score (log fibrosis score <0.60, corresponding to <1.8% fibrotic area); Low %HAA values; Absence of reticular, honeycombing, or traction bronchiectasis patterns on CT
Weak/non-specific features: %HAA had lower AUC (0.80) and lower specificity (62%) compared to CNN fibrosis score; %HAA not significantly associated with MUC5B risk variant; Male sex not independently associated with quantitative fibrosis score in regression; MUC5B variant association with deep-learning-detected subclinical fibrosis did not reach significance in subgroup analysis (p=0.18); TERT variant not associated with PrePF in this cohort
Interpretation notes: CNN fibrosis score classifies image regions as normal or fibrotic using pixel and texture features at multiple scales; trained on established IPF cases with more advanced disease than this cohort; Optimal log fibrosis score threshold of 0.60 (1.8% fibrotic area) gave 84% sensitivity and 86% specificity for visual PrePF diagnosis; Negative predictive value of CNN fibrosis score was 97%, making it more useful for ruling out fibrosis than confirming it (PPV only 46%); CNN fibrosis score outperformed %HAA in AUC (0.92 vs 0.80), accuracy, and specificity; Quantitative fibrosis score was positively associated with breathlessness even after controlling for age, sex, and smoking; Age was the strongest independent predictor of both visual and quantitative fibrosis; Smoking history had borderline association with quantitative fibrosis score (p=0.05)
